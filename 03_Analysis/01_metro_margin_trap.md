# Insight 1: The Metro Margin Trap

**Chart Type**: Combo Chart — Clustered Bars (Revenue) + Line with Markers (MOV)
**Deck Position**: Slide 3

---

## The Data

| City Tier | Total Revenue | Median Order Value |
|---|---|---|
| Tier A (Metro) | $1.31B | $7,931 |
| Tier B | $2.11B | $8,005 |
| Tier C (Suburban) | $1.66B | **$8,585** |

---

## The Finding

Tier A carries the premium operating costs of metropolitan commercial real estate — but generates the **lowest median ticket size across all three tiers**.

This is a unit economics inversion: the highest-cost geography is producing the weakest per-transaction value. Tier C suburban stores, which carry significantly lower operating cost assumptions, outperform Tier A by **$654 in median order value**.

The insight is not that Tier A has low revenue — Tier B has the highest total revenue. The insight is that Tier A underperforms on **both** axes simultaneously: lowest total volume AND lowest median ticket. For the tier with the highest assumed real estate overhead, this is a structural risk.

---

## Why a Combo Chart

| Question | Why Combo Wins |
|---|---|
| "Does high revenue = good unit economics?" | A single bar chart (revenue only) makes Tier B look best and Tier A look fine. Adding MOV as a line on the same chart forces both dimensions into one frame — the contradiction becomes visible. |
| "Is the Tier A problem scale or quality?" | Combo shows that even adjusting for revenue scale, Tier A's line marker sits lowest. It's not a volume problem — it's a ticket quality problem. |

**Why not a table**: Numbers without visual trajectory — the slope of the MOV line across A → B → C is the insight. A table requires mental arithmetic.

**Why median, not mean**: The dataset is right-skewed. Mean AOV would overstate all tiers by ~$1,216 on average. Median gives the typical transaction value without outlier distortion. The $654 gap between Tier A and Tier C ($7,931 vs $8,585) holds directionally under both measures.

---

## Important Caveat

This analysis has no cost data — operating costs for Tier A locations are **assumed** to be higher based on metropolitan commercial real estate conventions. The finding identifies a unit economics risk signal, not a confirmed profitability loss. The recommendation is to audit, not to assert.

---

## Action

- **Freeze Tier A footprint expansion** until operating cost data confirms or disproves the margin thesis
- Audit whether Tier A's higher transaction count (convenience volume) justifies the per-sqft real estate overhead
- Treat this as a signal that warrants CFO-level investigation, not a confirmed P&L conclusion
