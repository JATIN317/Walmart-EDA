# Insight 2: Product Cannibalization — Wallet Share Dilution

**Chart Type**: 100% Stacked Bar Chart — 3 tiers × 3 category segments
**Deck Position**: Slide 4

---

## The Data

| Category | Avg Transaction Value | Tier A Share | Tier B Share | Tier C Share |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Category 1 | $13,600 | 48.09% | 51.06% | **54.82%** |
| Category 5 | $6,200 | 27.13% | 25.81% | 23.57% |
| Category 8 | $7,500 | 24.78% | 23.13% | 21.61% |

---

## The Finding

Tier A and Tier C stores sell to similar demographic profiles — but their **product mix is structurally different**.

- **Tier A**: 51% of top-category wallet share goes to lower-ticket Categories 5 & 8 ($6k–$7.5k avg). Tier A operates as a **convenience center** — customers make quick, planned purchases of moderate-value items.
- **Tier C**: 55% of top-category wallet share concentrates in Category 1 ($13.6k avg). Tier C operates as a **premium destination** — customers arrive with intent to buy high-value items.

Category 1's average transaction value is roughly **2× that of Categories 5 and 8**. The wallet share difference compounds directly into the Tier A vs Tier C MOV gap identified in Insight 1.

This is termed "cannibalization" because low-ticket categories are consuming shelf prominence and customer attention that could otherwise convert toward premium Category 1 — not because products are substituting for each other in the traditional sense.

---

## Why a 100% Stacked Bar (Not a Regular Bar or Pie)

| Alternative | Why It Fails |
|---|---|
| Regular stacked bar | Tier B's higher total revenue would visually dominate. Tier A's 51% Category 5/8 share would be dwarfed in absolute terms. The composition difference — which is the finding — would be invisible. |
| Pie chart | Cannot compare proportions across 3 tiers side-by-side. Three separate pie charts require mental comparison — the shift from 48% → 55% in Category 1 across tiers is only visible with bars aligned on the same axis. |
| **100% stacked bar** ✓ | All tiers normalised to the same base. Category 1's growth from Tier A (48%) to Tier C (55%) is readable at a glance. The composition shift is the insight — stripping absolute scale is correct. |

---

## Note on Category Classification

"Category 1 = Premium" is an inference from transaction value ($13.6k average), not from the category name — which is masked. The relative value ranking (Category 1 >> Category 5, Category 8) is data-driven. The actual product contents of each category require a mapping table from the business before supply chain recommendations can be operationalised.

---

## Actions

- **Tier C**: Route deeper Category 1 inventory — the market exists and is already converting at 55% wallet share. Stockouts in premium categories are the primary risk.
- **Tier A**: Implement point-of-sale upselling tactics — place Category 1 items at eye level, introduce cross-sell prompts at checkout, and measure whether wallet share shifts toward Category 1 over the next campaign window.
- **Test**: Run a 90-day Tier A pilot with premium Category 1 placement. If Category 1 wallet share moves from 48% toward 52%+, the mechanism is store layout and placement, not inherent demographic difference.
